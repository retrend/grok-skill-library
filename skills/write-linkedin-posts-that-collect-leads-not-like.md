# Rule
Write the post your buyer would ask you to send them.

# Three rules behind every post
1. Give away the work: a finished thing usable the same day (skill pack, guide, checklist, template, repo, teardown).
2. Prove you used it yourself: name who on your team uses it and what it did for you.
3. Filter fast: line 1 states the problem or result so only your buyer stops. Everyone else scrolls.

# Inputs
- <LEAD_MAGNET>: a finished asset you use yourself
- <BUYER>: who you sell to
- <PROOF_NUMBER>: a real figure from your own work
- <EMAIL_CAPTURE_PAGE>: a page that asks for an email, not a comment

# Steps
1. Pick one post type below. Start with the behind-the-scenes demo.
2. Build one lead magnet you use yourself, finished and usable the same day.
3. Write line 1 for your buyer, with the proof or the problem in it.
4. Put the result or offer in line 2.
5. Link to <EMAIL_CAPTURE_PAGE> in the post. Don't ask for a comment word: the author reports LinkedIn now cuts reach on those (his test: 32 impressions in 18 minutes; unverified).
6. Follow up with people who fit while they still remember you. The author says signups without follow-up were wasted.
7. Track signups, meetings booked and revenue from content. Likes come last.
8. Reuse your best lead magnet about 3 months later with a new first line. Share it on X and YouTube too.

# The six post types
1. Behind-the-scenes demo: show your best person doing what you sell, with their exact tools. Order: person, result, list of jobs and tools.
```
<Name>, our <role>, <does the thing you sell> at <a real number>.
<What it changed for your business, in 1 line.>

What <the setup> does every week:
→ <job 1>
→ <job 2>
→ <job 3>

<The tools it runs on.>
Get the setup free: <EMAIL_CAPTURE_PAGE>
```
2. Guide that picks a fight: topic is the question your buyer asks right before they need you. Open with something they already dislike. Three bullets answer three objections (one per reader type). Show the result before the contents.
```
Rest in peace, <the thing your buyer is tired of>.
For 2026, I built <the guide> so you can <the result they want>.

→ <Objection 1, answered>
→ <Objection 2, answered>
→ <Objection 3, answered>

What our team got with it: <the result>.
What's inside: <the contents>
Get it free: <EMAIL_CAPTURE_PAGE>
```
3. Tool post: build something inside a tool your buyers use daily, then name every file or piece.
```
I built <what you built> that <result 1>, <result 2> and <result 3>, all inside <the tool your buyers use>.

The <N> files we use ourselves:
1. <name>
2. <name>
3. <name>
Get them free: <EMAIL_CAPTURE_PAGE>
```
4. Shift post: name what is changing in your buyer's job now. Old way in one line, new way in one line, then one step per line.
```
<The job> used to mean <the old way>.
<Who> do it like this now:
→ <step 1>
→ <step 2>
→ <step 3>
Get <the free thing that does step 1>: <EMAIL_CAPTURE_PAGE>
```
5. "My team didn't want me to share this": one-line story with tension, contents sorted by team or role, then say what it doesn't need (no setup, keys or code).
```
My team didn't want me to share this: <the thing we use for the job>.

For <team 1>: <item>
For <team 2>: <item>
For <team 3>: <item>

No setup. Copy it straight into <the tool they already use>.
Get it free: <EMAIL_CAPTURE_PAGE>
```
6. Link post: proof in line 1, offer in line 2, link to the email page, no comment word.
```
We packaged <N> <things> that <proof number> at <your company>.
Get them for free, ready to <use them>.

<What's inside, 1 line per item.>

<EMAIL_CAPTURE_PAGE>
```

# Success check
- Signups, meetings booked and revenue from content are tracked per post.
- A post can be judged a win with few likes if signups are high (the author's post #6: 186 likes, 142 day-one signups).

# Anti-patterns
- Posts with no asset behind them (the author's 2,400-like post produced 0 leads).
- Comment-word bait as the only capture method.
- Lead magnets that were generated and never used by your team.
- Collecting emails and never following up.
- Emailing or messaging signups beyond what they consented to; follow local email and privacy law. (added, not in source)
- Treating the author's counts as a benchmark for your account.
