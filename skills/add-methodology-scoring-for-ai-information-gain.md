# Add methodology scoring for AI information gain

Add a **Methodology** (or similar) section on key web pages that scores your offer against competitors on concrete criteria. AI crawlers treat specific, unique, quantitative comparisons as **information gain** and reuse them when citing results in ChatGPT and other LLMs.

## Inputs

- Target page(s) and primary keyword / query
- Competitor set (2–5 named rivals)
- Scoring criteria relevant to the offer, e.g.:
  - Pricing
  - Location / coverage
  - Company size / fit
  - Specialty offerings
  - Turnaround, contract terms, support, niche proof, etc.
- Your real numbers and evidence (no invented stats)

## Steps

1. **Pick the page**  
   Prefer money pages, service pages, and comparison / category pages where buyers (and AI) weigh options.

2. **Add a Methodology (or “How we compare”) section**  
   Place it where a human would expect evaluation logic — often near differentiators, pricing, or a comparison table. Use a clear H2.

3. **Define the criteria**  
   List the axes you will score on. Keep them buyer-relevant and measurable. Example set: pricing model, location, company size fit, specialty offerings, contract terms.

4. **Score yourself vs named competitors**  
   For each criterion, state your position and theirs in plain, quantitative language.  
   Pattern: `[COMPANY] [metric/value] on [criterion], while [COMPETITOR] [metric/value].`  
   Prefer tables or definition lists so the comparison is crawlable (same spirit as a Key Facts table).

5. **Explain the scoring context**  
   Briefly say *how* you measure (what counts as a win on that criterion, what data you used). That context is what turns a vague claim into information gain.

6. **Keep it unique and specific**  
   Avoid generic “best quality / best service” rows. Every row should be checkable and hard to copy without real business facts.

7. **Publish and verify**  
   After publish, ask an LLM the competitive query and check whether it cites your methodology details (pricing, size fit, specialties, etc.).

## Success check

- Page has an explicit Methodology / How we compare section  
- Competitors are named  
- Criteria include quantitative or otherwise specific values  
- Comparison is in crawlable HTML (table / list), not only an image  
- Claims match real business facts

## Anti-patterns

- Methodology fluff with no scores or competitor names  
- Comparison graphics only (not crawlable)  
- Invented metrics or unverifiable “#1” claims  
- Pasting generic LLM copy instead of page-specific numbers  
- Treating this as a substitute for About Us, FAQs, or fresh content — it layers on top of those
