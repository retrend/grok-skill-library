---
name: Reverse-engineer a competitor's Google Ads weekly
description: >-
  Use this when you already run Google Ads and want a weekly process to see who
  bids against you, what ads they run, which keywords they abandoned and where
  they are weak by location, then turn those gaps into campaign tests.
---
Weekly cadence. The source says a first pass takes about 10 minutes (author's claim, not verified).

You need your own Google Ads account for Auction Insights. Semrush Advertising Research (paid) is used in step 3.

## Inputs
- Your Google Ads account, with Auction Insights on the campaigns or ad groups that matter
- One competitor domain to start with, taken from Auction Insights
- Google Ads Transparency Center: https://adstransparency.google.com
- Semrush, if you use step 3

## Steps
1. **Auction Insights, not guesses.** Open Auction Insights on your top campaigns and ad groups. Note the exact domains bidding against you on your most valuable keywords, who outranks you, overlap rate and who is gaining impression share. That list is who you watch.
2. **Google Ads Transparency Center.** Enter the competitor's domain. Review the display, video, shopping and search ads running now. Study messaging, offers and creative approach. You are not copying; you are asking how they say things.
   - Generic ads: an opening to be more targeted.
   - Polished, targeted messaging: a clear strategy you need to match.
3. **Abandoned keywords in Semrush (paid).** Advertising Research > Position Changes > Lost. These are keywords they used to bid on and dropped. Possible reasons:
   - Not profitable for their model (you just saved time and money).
   - Budget moved elsewhere (a chance to take that share).
   - Dropped by mistake (the author says this happens more than people think).
   Also check which landing pages get the most paid traffic (homepage, collection pages, product pages, quiz funnels). That is their acquisition funnel. Ask where you can be better.
4. **Geographic gaps.** In the Transparency Center, use the location filter to compare countries, states and cities. Author's example: an aggressive free-gift offer in New Zealand but passive ads in Australia. Where they are weak is your opening.
5. **Stack the findings and look for what they are not doing.** Author's examples:
   - No video or image ads: test Demand Gen and Performance Max.
   - Generic white-background product shots: use lifestyle images.
   - Weak titles: rewrite with better keywords.
6. **Do one today, then repeat.** Pick one competitor, run the process, document it, launch better campaigns from it, then move to the next competitor.

## Success check
- A written brief for one competitor: Auction Insights, current ads, abandoned keywords (if Semrush used), landing-page funnel, location gaps.
- The brief names at least one thing they are not doing and a campaign change you will test.
- None of their copy, trademarks or claims ends up in your ads. (added, not in source)

## Anti-patterns
- Guessing who competes with you instead of reading Auction Insights.
- Copying their ads or offers.
- Treating a "Lost" keyword as a sure win; it may be unprofitable for you too.
- Checking one country only and missing a weaker region.
- Using this as a stored creative library; for a first-seen/last-seen ad catalogue use build-a-competitor-ad-spy-catalog-for-the-creati. (added, not in source)
