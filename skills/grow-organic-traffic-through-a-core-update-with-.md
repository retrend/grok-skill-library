# Grow organic traffic through a core update with eight foundation moves

Noel Ceta's eight-step playbook for a site that kept climbing through Google's November 2026 core update. AI does the typing: code, drafts written to a spec, translations, and checks against the spec. People decide which searches matter, which page answers each one, what the spec is, and what never ships.

## Steps

1. Match the homepage to the exact search. Use the exact phrase people type as the homepage H1 and title. Turn the H2s into the questions people ask, and add an FAQ. (His went from 10 to 17 questions.)
2. Fix the basics before writing anything new. Crawl your own site and check that every page has one H1, menus are real links rather than JavaScript buttons, and pages are prerendered. (One of his pages had 11 H1s. Response time dropped from over 0.5 seconds to 0.1 to 0.2 seconds.)
3. Remove duplicate pages. Find pages that share a title or meta description. Give each one its own URL, title and description, and redirect the old URLs. (He had 22 product pages sharing one title.)
4. Give every comparison search its own page. Write the spec first: the sections, FAQs, internal links and a verdict. Then build every page to that spec so none ends up thin. (He built 136 pages to one spec: 8 sections, 6 FAQs and at least 8 internal links each.)
5. Build hubs. Group related pages under a hub that has a guide section and an FAQ, and show related posts under every blog post so no page sits on its own. (He built 16 hubs of 1,500 to 1,900 words each.)
6. Publish data nobody else has, such as your users' results, your own numbers or your own tool. If Google can get it elsewhere, it has no reason to rank you. (His is a tool showing live averages from his own users.) He calls this the step competitors can't copy.
7. Translate what already works. In Search Console, find the countries already showing impressions. Translate your proven pages for them, add hreflang, then have someone review the translations. (He runs 15 languages, and one review pass fixed about 1,800 strings.)
8. Keep the blog going. Real people write it, on a schedule, at a pace you can keep up for a year.
9. Divide the work. AI writes the code, drafts to your spec and translations, and checks every page against the spec. You decide the target searches, which page answers each one, the spec itself, and the never-ship list (for example, made-up statistics).
10. Success check (added, not in source): a fresh crawl shows one H1 per page and no duplicate titles or descriptions. Every comparison page passes the spec check. Search Console clicks and impressions are tracked weekly from before the change.

## Anti-patterns

- Writing new content before fixing H1s, links, rendering and duplicates.
- Building comparison pages without a spec, which gives thin, uneven pages.
- Orphan pages with no hub and no related links.
- Publishing machine translations without a human review.
- Letting AI decide strategy, or publish made-up statistics.
- A burst of posts for one week, then silence.
