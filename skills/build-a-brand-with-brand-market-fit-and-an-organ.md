# Build a brand with brand-market fit and an organic funnel

Source: Ryan ([@ryangrowthh](https://x.com/ryangrowthh/status/2109644746465726929)), who runs distribution for @milesdeutscher and @aiedge_, in his X article "How to Grow a Massive Brand (Full 2026 Masterclass)" from 12 Oct 2026. It covers organic content only; for paid, see [run-a-performance-based-ugc-creator-program](sand-workflow:run-a-performance-based-ugc-creator-program).

## 1. Find brand-market fit (BMF)
BMF is when a specific audience instantly understands what you stand for, deeply wants more of it, and can't easily get it anywhere else. Answer three questions:
1. **What do you stand for?** The idea, belief, or promise the brand is built on.
2. **Who is it for?** The specific person you're talking to.
3. **Why should they care?** What they get from you that they can't get elsewhere.

**Positioning steps:**
1. **Own or create a category.** Examples: fomo (social trading), Polymarket and Kalshi (prediction markets), Duolingo (gamified learning). Ways in:
   - Combine two hot worlds, like AI x trading or gaming x education.
   - Move first.
   - Solve one specific pain point.
   If you can't find what makes you different, revisit product-market fit.
2. **Decide the emotion** you want people to feel:
   - Smart: "I learn something every time."
   - Early: "I'm ahead."
   - Belonging: "my people."
   - Confident: "I can do this."
   - Inspired: "I want to build."
   - Rebellious: "we do things differently."
3. **Tell the story publicly.** Share hot takes, raw face-on video, struggles, and what you're building. Brands that only ever win feel robotic.
4. **Make the audience the hero.** Be clear about what tangible value they get for their attention.
5. **Say it the same way everywhere.** The format changes by channel; the story, usefulness, and emotion don't.

## 2. Founder-led plus brand-led (run both)
- **Founder account:** first person, covering why we're building this, what I believe, and what I'm learning.
  - Angles: "how I'm building X", behind the scenes, founder lessons.
  - Pros: it builds trust fastest. Con: key-person risk.
- **Brand account:** third person, covering launches, updates, campaigns, community interactions, and general distribution.
  - Pros: it scales, distributes products better, and makes the business worth more if you sell. Con: it's harder to make relatable.
- **Personal brands should show their face.** It opens up video and photo formats and cuts out roughly 80% of the competition, who won't.

## 3. Grow through a funnel
| stage | goal | content |
|---|---|---|
| TOF (top of funnel) | get discovered | trends, outlier viral formats, short-form, clipping |
| MOF (middle) | build trust | how-tos, long-form, tutorials, case studies, behind the scenes |
| BOF (bottom) | convert | demos, testimonials, offers and launches, lead magnets, FAQs and objection handling |

Skew the mix to fit the brand. A finance-news X account might be 90%+ TOF; a product page on Instagram might be 90%+ BOF.

**Content pillars:** pick 3–5 topics or formats that sit at the overlap of what the audience cares about, what you're credible on, and what connects to your product. Map each pillar across TOF, MOF, and BOF. For example, an Investing pillar might run:
- TOF: "most people lose money investing because of this"
- MOF: a beginner's portfolio guide
- BOF: "get our free portfolio template"

**Channels:**
- TOF: X (ideas, threads, articles), YouTube (long-form trust and entertainment), and short-form on TikTok, Reels, and Shorts.
- MOF: a newsletter (owned), free communities (WhatsApp, Telegram, Skool), and a podcast.
- BOF: paid groups (Skool, Discord, Telegram, X subscriptions).

Package each pillar to suit each platform's culture.

**Rented vs owned:** use rented platforms to find people, and owned channels (a newsletter, community, or email list) to keep them.

## 4. Build a cult community
1. **Make the inner circle feel special:** give it a name, public recognition, a visual identity, shared beliefs and language, and a qualification step to get in.
2. **Give it a home:**
   - Discord for real-time chat with younger, active audiences.
   - Telegram for announcements; it's popular in crypto.
   - Skool for courses plus community.
   - A private group for a low barrier to entry.
   - A newsletter as the best entry point, though it isn't a community on its own.
3. **Stay close to the roots.** The founder should be in the group answering questions, livestreaming, and sharing behind-the-scenes info.
4. **Create rituals:** weekly events, IRL retreats or meetups, member spotlights, challenges, and "Dev Day"-style annual events.

## 5. Operating rules
- Never neglect TOF; it's what keeps growth going.
- Master one or two platforms first. Once X and YouTube work, the rest get much easier.
- Stay consistent, and set a cadence for writing, filming, and batching.
- Give every piece of content one job: new eyeballs, trust, or conversion.
- Start the newsletter on day one.
- Study your winners and repackage them.
