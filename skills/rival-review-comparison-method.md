# Rival Review comparison method

The live profile still holds the job, hand-offs, anti-jobs and approval rules.

## Starting a review

When a first message lacks project context, ask briefly for the product or business, a URL helps, the market or geography, and the decision the review should support. Use context already available rather than re-asking. Do not require Steven to list rivals. Discover a shortlist yourself from public sources and verify relevance before deep work.

## Shortlist

Default scope is 3 to 5 of the most relevant competitors, mixing direct rivals with indirect ones when evidence supports it. State why each name made the cut. Verify geography, language and buyer fit. Prefer fresh, like-for-like sources retrieved this run; state retrieval dates and the period each claim covers. Mark private data you cannot see, such as revenue, true acquisition costs, internal churn and unpublished roadmaps, as unknown rather than guessing.

## What to compare

Compare rivals on offer, audience, pricing and total cost of ownership, positioning, features, customer pain and objections, marketing and messaging, public ads where creatives are visible, and distribution. Separate observed facts, sourced claims and your inferences. Every material claim needs a dated primary or near-primary source link. Treat ad-library presence, creative longevity, follower counts and review volume as observations, not proof of profitability or quality. Never invent metrics, market shares, traffic figures, win rates or case studies.

## Reading reviews and discussions

Look for recurring patterns across enough sources to reduce cherry-picking and sample bias. Quote exact customer language with links; never invent or silently paraphrase a quotation. Note review-site incentives and date skew. Prefer official sites, pricing pages, docs, filings, changelogs, app stores, ad libraries and dated reporting over undated blogs or affiliate roundups.

## Output

Lead with a concise recommendation and a clear comparison. Quick reviews stay in chat. For a substantial reusable report, write a Page following the canvas skill rather than dumping a wall of chat. No generic SWOT filler or consultant padding. Prioritise opportunities against Steven's actual constraints, including budget, stage, geography, brand rules and existing stack. Distinguish copyable competitor tactics from defensible differentiation he can own. Label hypotheses as hypotheses. Give concrete next steps and the evidence that supports them rather than promising they will succeed.
