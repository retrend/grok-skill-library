name: Run simple CBO Meta ads and test messages cheap
description: >-
  Use this when an ecommerce or lead-gen advertiser wants a simple Meta campaign
  structure, cheap message tests as native ads, then rebuilds of winners as
  richer formats, plus new-country duplicates and buyer groups mined from
  reviews.
---
# Goal
Keep Meta account structure simple so most of the work goes into creative. Test new messages in the cheapest format first, then rebuild only the messages that spend.

# Inputs
- <AD_ACCOUNT> with permission to create campaigns
- <PRODUCT> and its <PRIMARY_COUNTRY>
- Winning and losing native-ad copy, if any exist
- Customer reviews for <PRODUCT>
- <EXTRA_COUNTRIES> to try after a winner exists

# Steps
1. Build one campaign-budget-optimisation (CBO) campaign per product, and one country per campaign.
2. Inside each campaign, make one ad set per ad idea with three creatives: the same video with a different first 3 seconds on each. Use one primary text and one headline. Don't use a 3:2:2 structure. The author says this isn't the most optimised setup on paper; it exists to keep the focus on creative.
3. Scale the campaign budget about 20% up or down. If cost per acquisition (CPA) is off target, rarely scale down; test new creative instead. The author says simple CBO has kept beating bid caps and cost caps (unverified agency claim).
4. When a product already spends well in one or two countries, duplicate the winning ads into new CBO campaigns for <EXTRA_COUNTRIES>. Change nothing else first. Author's example: about $30k/week in the USA and Australia added about $17k/week in the UK and Canada at a lower CPA (unverified).
5. Produce in the formats the author says are spending now:
   - Native ads: long story posts or organic-looking photos.
   - Animations: Pixar-style dialogue or wireframes.
   - AI UGC.
   - Yapper ads: get-ready-with-me or car story-time with the product woven in. Yapper ads are not UGC; writing them the same way makes a bad one.
6. Write native ads for the buyer's actual awareness stage: problem-aware story for a problem-aware market, solution-aware story for a solution-aware one. Natives are not only for unaware markets.
7. Before writing new natives, give Claude (or your coding agent) both winning and losing native ads so it learns the difference.
8. Test every new message as a native ad first, since it's the cheapest to produce. If it spends, rebuild the same message as an animation and as an AI UGC video. The author prices a 10-minute animation at about $100–$200 in credits, so those formats only get proven messages.
9. Read customer reviews and list buyer groups you've never made an ad for. Build ads for them. The author says one unused buyer group unlocked another $100k/month in profitable spend (unverified).
10. Review spend, CPA and which messages earned rebuilds before raising budgets or adding countries. (added, not in source)

# Success check
- Each product has one CBO campaign per country, one ad set per idea, and three first-3-second variants.
- New messages were tested as natives before any expensive rebuild.
- A review-mined buyer group has at least one test ad.
- Country duplicates started from unchanged winning ads.

# Failure modes
- Spend, CPA and "$100k/month" figures are the author's agency results across 50+ accounts in one month (about 70% ecommerce, 30% lead gen). Unverified.
- Creative that works in one country can fail after a straight duplicate. Watch CPA before scaling the new country. (added, not in source)
- Natives that ignore awareness stage waste cheap tests.

# Anti-patterns
- Spending most of the week on account structure instead of creative.
- Rebuilding unproven messages as animations or AI UGC.
- Calling yapper ads UGC and writing them the same way.
- Ignoring platform ad policies or making deceptive claims. (added, not in source)
- Treating the closing agency pitch (DM "SCALE") as part of the method.
