# Build and test small Roblox games with Claude

Make simple Roblox games with Claude writing the scripts. Ship each one, test it with a small ad spend, then kill it or scale it. Each new game reuses scripts from the last one.

Inputs: Roblox Studio, Claude, about 1 hour a day, and $50–100 ad budget per game.

## Steps

1. Find a genre that's still open.
   - Open Roblox's Charts and look at Up-and-Coming.
   - Look for games under 3 months old with 1,000+ players.
   - New games breaking into a genre means it's still open. If the top 10 are all 5 years old, skip that genre.
2. Play the top 3 games in that genre for 10 minutes each.
   - Write down everything that's slow, confusing or boring.
   - That list is your game design. You're building the same kind of game without the annoying parts.
3. Describe your game in one sentence.
   - Too vague: "an obby."
   - Good: "escape a cage that gets harder every level, with friends."
   - If you can't explain it in one sentence, players won't get it in 10 seconds.
4. Get a build plan from Claude (about 15 minutes).
   - Give Claude the one-sentence idea and your list of fixes.
   - Ask for a build plan split into small systems: movement, levels, UI, saving, shop.
   - The plan is your to-do list.
   - Example prompt (added, not in source; the post gives no wording):
     ```
     Game idea: <ONE_SENTENCE_IDEA>
     Problems to fix from the top games in this genre: <FIX_LIST>
     Write a build plan for this Roblox game, split into small systems I can build one per session: movement, levels, UI, saving, shop. For each system, list what it does and how I'll test it in Studio.
     ```
5. Build one system per session.
   - Claude writes the script. You paste it into Studio and test it.
   - If something breaks, paste the error back to Claude to fix.
   - At an hour a day, expect 2–3 weeks for a simple game. The author's fastest went from first line of code to live in under 24 hours.
6. Spend extra time on the first 30 seconds.
   - Most players decide straight away whether to stay.
   - Give them a clear goal and fast action, with no confusing menus.
   - The author says an hour here is worth more than an hour on any other part of the game.
7. Make a thumbnail people want to click.
   - Study the thumbnails of the top 5 games in your genre.
   - Match their style and colours, but make it your own.
   - On Roblox the thumbnail is your ad. A great game with a bad thumbnail gets no players.
8. Add ways to earn before launch, then test.
   - Game passes.
   - In-game items and boosts.
   - Rewarded video ads.
   - Publish, put $50–100 into Roblox ads, and watch for 3–5 days.
9. Kill it or scale it.
   - If players leave fast, drop the game and start the next one.
   - If players stay and come back, push updates and spend more on ads.
10. Repeat in the same genre.
    - The next game reuses about half the scripts from the last one, so each game gets quicker to build.
    - Hits fade over time, so keep shipping.

## Success check

- A one-sentence pitch and a fix list exist before any code is written.
- Every system was tested in Studio before the next one was started.
- After the 3–5 day ad test, there's a written kill-or-scale decision based on whether players stayed and came back.

## The author's income maths (unverified)

- His game made $778 in one day with 3,000 players. He works that out as about 26 cents per player.
- He then says a small game that "holds 100 players" makes about $26 a day, or $780 a month, so 10 such games make $7,800 a month.
- Treat this as a claim. It's one game's best day. "3,000 players" and "holds 100 players" probably measure different things (players in a day versus players online at once), so the maths may not carry over. He sells a course, linked at the end of the thread.

## Anti-patterns

- Inventing a new genre instead of improving one that's already working.
- Picking a genre where the top 10 games are years old.
- Building the whole game in one go instead of one tested system at a time.
- Launching with no way to earn, or with a thumbnail you didn't study.
- Holding on to a game that players leave quickly.
- Planning around $7,800 a month. The post itself says not every game will hit.
